Project 05 / Selected Work

Two ways of living, one mountain landscape.

Dharamshala is presented as a completed project combining two distinct accommodation directions: forest cottages and hybrid houses. The project language is rooted in the supplied visual — compact timber-clad volumes, pitched roofs, generous openings and a close relationship with the pine landscape.

Rather than forcing both formats into one architectural expression, the case study treats them as two related typologies designed for different ways of inhabiting the same mountain setting.
01 / Two Project Types

Cottages + Hybrid Houses.

The Dharamshala project can be presented as a small family of structures rather than a single building. The two types share the same landscape-first character while serving different spatial requirements.

TYPE 01 / COTTAGES

Forest Cottages

Compact retreat units designed to sit lightly among the trees. The visual language focuses on warm timber surfaces, simple pitched forms, private outlooks and a strong sense of being inside the forest.

Hybrid Houses

A larger residential format combining the warmth of a mountain cabin with more generous living areas, outdoor connections and flexible spaces for longer stays or family use.

03 / Design Approach

Build less like a resort. Live more like a mountain home.

The supplied image suggests a deliberately low-key architectural language: timber-clad volumes, simple roof geometries and small building footprints distributed through an existing pine landscape.

For the case-study story, the cottages and hybrid houses are presented as a coordinated system — allowing the project to offer different scales of stay without losing a consistent visual identity.
